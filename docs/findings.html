Beyond Earth · 813 Challenge Proof of Concept findings Wadi as-Sirhan, Al Jouf · Saudi Arabia · Feb–Sep 2026

A satellite can see the crop is gone. It cannot see who took it.

Alfalfa in Wadi as-Sirhan is cut every thirty days. A mown field and a dying field read almost identically from orbit — which means the obvious way to rank fields for irrigation sends water to the fields that least need it. We measured how often that happens, and built the fix.

The confound

Vegetation here is a sawtooth, not a trend

Every cut drops a field from roughly 0.90 NDVI to roughly 0.35 within ten days. Then it regrows, and thirty days later it is cut again. On any given date a substantial share of fields are mid-cycle and look exactly like severe water stress.

Rank by decline and you are not ranking by stress. You are ranking by how recently the mower came through.

The measurement

Most of what a decline ranking flags has simply been mown

We classified every pivot using its own NDVI trajectory: healthy, freshly cut and recovering, or low and staying low. The cut group is the trap — those fields sit at the top of a decline ranking and need nothing. Taking the twenty fields such a ranking would send you to first, and counting how many had simply been harvested, gives the same answer at every date in the season.

16 of the 20 fields a decline ranking sends you to first had just been harvested.

Measured at eight dates across the season, and never fewer than eleven of twenty. At season end that ranking and ours agree on one field out of twenty. In an aquifer that does not recharge, that is the number worth fixing.

The tool

Judge each field against its own rhythm

Instead of asking whether a field has fallen, we ask whether it has fallen further than it usually does at this point in its own cutting cycle, and whether it has failed to come back when it was due.

  • 1Find the cuts. A local NDVI maximum counts as a regrowth peak only if it stands 0.20 above the low points on both sides. That prominence test separates a real cut from a cloudy pixel.
  • 2Measure the field's own cycle. Median spacing between its peaks, bounded to 18–60 days. Fewer than two clean peaks and the field is flagged as having no observed cycle, rather than given a false one.
  • 3Expected value at phase. For a field d days past its last cut, what did this same field read d days after each of its previous cuts? A low-vigour field is judged against itself.
  • 4Priority = deficit × hectares. How far behind its own history, weighted by how much land that covers. Exactly zero for a field that is on schedule, so a freshly cut field can never enter the queue.

On cycle

“Cut 12 days ago on a 31-day cycle. The drop is on schedule — this is harvest, not stress.” Priority 0.

Overdue

“Cut 44 days ago on a 31-day cycle and still at NDVI 0.28. It should have regrown by now — inspect.” Priority = deficit × area.

The hyperspectral question

We tested our own best idea, and it failed

Our original claim was that narrow bands — the photochemical reflectance index at 531/570 nm, the red-edge position, the 970 nm water-absorption depth — could separate a cut field from a stressed one where NDVI cannot. Sentinel-2 has none of those bands. NASA's EMIT does.

We set it up as a prediction contest rather than a significance test: labels taken from what each field's NDVI did 15–45 days after the hyperspectral measurement, rows restricted to fields already low on the day, folds split by field, twenty repeats.

Only two EMIT granules over this area matched a Sentinel-2 day within two days, and a February acquisition does not look like a July one. With two scenes, random folds cannot separate plant physiology from atmospheric conditions. We report the narrow-band indices per field as measurement, and we rank nothing with them.

The bottleneck is scene count, not field count: 2 of 60 EMIT granules over this area met the matching rule. Leave-one-scene-out needs six to eight before it has any power. That is worth doing. It is not worth blocking a submission on, and the organisers have confirmed hyperspectral use is bonus credit rather than a requirement.

Honest limits

What this is not

Not a diagnosis

No field visit, no irrigation log, no yield record. Every status is a screening result. The tool says “look at this field first”, never “this field is water-stressed”.

Needs two cuts

A field with fewer than two clean peaks in the window gets the regional 30-day default and is flagged as such. Newly planted fields are the weak case.

Detection is one date

Fields bare on the chosen day are absent from the set. On an earlier run, detecting in July found 328 fields rather than 511 — that gap is the size of the effect.

Relative, not absolute

A field declining across several cycles is judged against its own already-degraded history and will under-flag. A longer baseline fixes it.
